Local Engineer Mistakes Weekend for Sprint, Ships Anyway

An ambitious homepage, a Kubernetes cluster too many, and a household grown suspicious of the YAML files multiplying in the hallway closet.

By our Workbench Correspondent · Filed at some hour past midnight

In an unassuming basement beneath the Rotterdam suburbs, a developer has spent the weekend producing roughly nothing anyone asked for, and yet somehow too much of it. Sources describe the output as “surprisingly functional” and the developer as “still talking about GitOps at dinner.”

At the centre of the operation is Nerdy Terminal, a keyboard-first tab-agent manager that allows the user to switch between sessions without — critically — ever moving a single centimetre of the right hand. “Why would I,” the developer was quoted as saying, while allegedly toggling between nine sessions with a shortcut he’d forgotten he’d bound.

Officials at the household level have raised concerns over what they describe as the “creeping YAML problem,” noting that Home Assistant entities have now outnumbered light switches in the home by a factor of roughly four. The developer maintains that this is, in fact, the point.

The lights, for the record, do work. Everything else is a matter of ongoing negotiation.

Domestic Life · Page A3

Home Assistant’s WAF Reaches Historic Low

Our Home Correspondent

The Wife Acceptance Factor of a locally-operated Home Assistant installation has, according to independent observers, reached what analysts describe as dangerously low levels, despite the homeowner’s repeated insistence that YAML-based automations are “the future of living.”

The system, held together primarily by Zigbee and stubbornness, continues to operate on a budget of spare evenings and a steady supply of replacement batteries for motion sensors that nobody asked to install.

The lights work. Everything else is negotiable.
