04 / why now
// Timing matters

Why this works now.

Three curves crossed in the last 18 months. The cost of building has collapsed. The cost of integrating just did the same. Distribution stayed roughly free.

2018 — context

Containers everywhere

Kubernetes wins. One person can run a fleet, not just a server. Infrastructure stops being a team sport.

2022

LLMs go general

Code generation crosses the "actually useful" line. The bottleneck moves from typing to taste.

2024

MCP standardizes

Agents finally speak the same protocol as your tools. Glue code becomes a config file.

2026 — now

One nerd, full stack

Solo builders ship what used to take a team. Leverage is no longer scarce; conviction is.

07 / unfair advantage
// Why this is hard to copy

The moat is
taste × throughput.

A compounding combination: opinionated defaults, a personal toolchain that erases setup, and the discipline to delete more than you ship.

M/01

The toolchain compounds

Every project sharpens the next. A new product spins up in hours, not weeks, because the boring layer is already paid for.

M/02

End-to-end ownership

One brain holds frontend, backend, infra, and ops. No handoff cost. The decision and the diff happen in the same tab.

M/03

Opinionated defaults

Strong taste rules out 80% of the design space upfront. What survives is small, fast, and doesn't apologize for itself.

M/04

Discipline to delete

The product gets better the more you remove. Shipping less is shipping a moat — competitors keep adding features.
