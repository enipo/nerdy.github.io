Toward a Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Operator Framework for Lightly-Supervised Engineering Workloads

Abstract § 1

We present nerdy.nl, an opinionated framework for orchestrating heterogeneous AI agents over real engineering tasks across cloud, edge, and domestic infrastructure. Building on the tmux-based Nerdy Terminal , we evaluate a three-tier supervisor → tab-agent → worker topology against single-agent baselines on maintenance, deployment, and incident-response workloads. Across 4 production clusters and 6 long-running services, the proposed framework reduces mean-time-to-resolution by 61.4% while keeping operator cognitive load below the self-reported tolerance threshold . We further argue, from 200+ Home Assistant entities and a custom MCP gateway , that the economics of tinkering at scale have shifted: a single engineer can now own a stack that, a decade ago, would have required a team.

1. Introduction § 2

The modern software engineer operates at the intersection of distributed systems, machine learning, and physical computing. Prior work has shown that the bottleneck is no longer compute, storage, or even talent — it is coordination. We hypothesise H1: that a layered, message-passing agent topology, executed by a single human operator, can sustain production-grade SLOs across an arbitrarily heterogeneous fleet, provided the message channel is durable and observable.

3. Methods § 4

Tasks T are decomposed into a partially ordered DAG and dispatched to workers wi. The expected completion cost for a task set is modelled as:

C(T) = Σi   ci + λ · ⟨coord⟩ · log n (1)

where n is worker count and ⟨coord⟩ the empirical coordination overhead. We measure ⟨coord⟩ as the wall-clock fraction spent in tm peek and approval loops. The operator's attention budget A is bounded by Miller's law :

A ≤ 7 ± 2   concurrent contexts (2)

5. Key Findings § 6

  1. Three-tier agent topology dominated single-agent baselines on every workload tested .
  2. Coordination cost grows sub-linearly in n when channels are durable and approvals are explicit .
  3. GitOps reconciliation eliminated 100% of observed configuration drift incidents over 9 months .
  4. Local-first home automation reduced cloud-API dependency by 87%, with no observable regression in user (resident) satisfaction.
  5. The tinkerer-as-platform-engineer regime is now economically viable for one operator.

8. Discussion § 9

The framework's main limitation is the operator: there is exactly one, and the operator requires sleep. Future work will explore replication of the operator via offline checkpoints (cf. coffee), and a stricter formal model for ⟨coord⟩ under adversarial Slack notifications . Threats to validity include selection bias (the author chose the workloads) and survivorship bias (failed clusters were quietly removed from the dataset).
