— The Feature —

A Man, Five Kubernetes Clusters, and a Question.

An intimate portrait of what happens when a developer builds his own control plane because the existing ones were — apparently — insufficient.

He stares at the terminal for thirty-seven seconds before saying it. “GitOps,” he says, barely above a whisper, and the matter appears to be settled. On his desk: a mechanical keyboard, two coffees at different stages of cooling, and a Raspberry Pi running something he cannot quite remember the purpose of. The clusters hum. The clusters do not need to hum, but they do.

“It's not about necessity,” he explains. “It's about form.”

Department · Software

Nerdy Terminal: A Defense of the Keyboard.

A manifesto in the form of a macOS utility. Multiple sessions. Instant switching. The subject refuses — under any circumstance — to move his hand thirty centimetres to the right. “Why would I?” he asks, as though the question answers itself. It does.

02
Department · AI & Infrastructure

On the MCP Gateway, or: the Bridge to Everything.

Slack. Jira. Grafana. Tailscale. A single gateway permits agentic models to converse with each of them. The effect is not dissimilar to that of a concierge, should a concierge also write YAML and occasionally return HTTP 504.

03
Department · Games

Pixel Tanks & the Fine Art of Losing Friends.

A top-down multiplayer project, rendered in a resolution so low as to seem deliberate. It is deliberate. Survivors report intact friendships; the rest report they are simply “taking a break.”

“Putting the Y in Nerdy — as in: why, dear god, why?”

Dispatch — The BasementSawdust & Ambition.

The workshop exists, currently, in the form of a plan and a pile. A table saw has been purchased and justified as “an investment.” So has the router. So has the second clamp, and the third. “One can never have enough clamps,” the subject says, in what appears to be a direct quotation from every woodworker ever.

Future issues will report on progress, should any occur.

Family Life — Dad × 3Three Kids, One Smart Bulb.

The Wife Acceptance Factor of the household's Home Assistant installation is, at present, “dangerously low.” The Child Acceptance Factor is surprisingly high, provided the children are permitted to change the bedroom lights to pink via Siri. They are.

“They don't care about clusters,” the subject notes. “They care about colours.”
