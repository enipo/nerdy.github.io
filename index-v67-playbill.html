The Programme

three acts, two intermissions, one lukewarm coffee

Act the First
In Which Our Hero Refuses the Mouse
A dimly lit study. Upon the desk: Nerdy Terminal, a keyboard-first session manager for macOS. The protagonist glances at his trackpad as at a rival. “Why would I move my hand thirty centimetres to the right,” he declares, “when I could press two keys?” Nine sessions switch instantly. The audience gasps politely.
Act the Second
The Over‑Engineering of a Domestic Life
Lights rise on a rack of humming Kubernetes clusters, wildly excessive for one household. Enter MCP Gateway stage left, bridging agents to Slack, Jira, databases, Grafana. Enter Home Assistant, held aloft by Zigbee and stubbornness alone. The WAF drops. The lights dim. Our hero whispers: “GitOps.”
Act the Third
Of Drums, Sawdust, and Small Humans
A basement workshop, mostly shavings and aspiration. A drum kit in the corner, occasionally in rhythm. Pixel Tanks rumble across a distant CRT — friendships tested in low resolution. Three children enter; they do not care about the clusters. They do care about the smart lights. Curtain.

Director’s Note

a word from the stage

This production began, as so many do, with a wife who asked what he was building. He could not explain. He gestured vaguely at a terminal, muttered about YAML, and promised the lights would still work. They did. Mostly.

What you are about to witness is not a website. It is a workbench on which things are made, broken, and occasionally shipped: a terminal manager, a bridge between AI and everything, a tank game played in pixels, a smart home held together by Zigbee and hope.

There will be no thought leadership. There will be no content strategy. Everything worth saying has already been said, probably by a large language model, somewhere on the internet, at 3 a.m. This is simply a playbill for a man who keeps tinkering because the alternative is sitting still.

The drums are real. The kids are real. The clusters, regrettably, are also real. Enjoy the show.
